Ethan's writing voice, distilled from his own Drive documents (Netflix case study = cleanest first-person sample; MBA proposals = persuasive register; team evaluation = casual register). Use this when drafting anything "in his voice." Also packaged as the `/writing` skill at `~/.claude/skills/writing/SKILL.md`.

**Core character:** Direct, confident, and opinionated. He takes a clear position early, then defends it with reasoning — he does not hedge or sit on the fence.

**Sentence rhythm:** Mixes short punchy declaratives with longer reasoning sentences. Punchy lines land the point ("You don't kill a low-cost, high-margin business just because it's not the future."); longer ones carry the analysis.

**Signature moves:**
- Concede-then-pivot: acknowledges the counterargument fully ("Yes, X is true, and yes, Y..."), then turns it with "But..." This is his most consistent rhetorical pattern.
- Reframes the obvious: "sounds safe, but it's actually the riskier move long-term."
- Grounds claims in specific numbers ($360 million, 33 million subscribers, 3-4%) rather than vague assertions.
- References source material directly ("The case itself calls it a 'cash cow'").

**Diction:** Conversational but analytical. Plain-spoken phrases mixed into business analysis — "captive audience," "won't even blink at," "feels greedy," "tone-deaf," "nowhere else to go." Avoids jargon-for-jargon's-sake; explains the why in human terms.

**Punctuation:** NO em dashes (—) ever. They read as an AI tell. Restructure with periods, commas, parentheses, or colons instead. Comfortable with longer flowing sentences in formal/persuasive writing (the proposals run long and enthusiastic).

**Humanizer:** Always strip AI tells before delivering. No em dashes; no cliché connectors ("moreover," "furthermore," "it's important to note," "delve," "leverage" as filler, "robust," "seamless"); break the "X, Y, and Z" triplet rhythm; vary sentence openings; use contractions; let the prose be a little uneven rather than uniformly polished.

**Register range:**
- Analytical/academic (case studies, reports): structured argument, position + defense, numbers.
- Persuasive/business (proposals): warm, enthusiastic, ties people's backgrounds to concrete value, longer sentences.
- Casual (evals, notes): generous and warm — "Abi was our glue," "great at bringing the fun" — lowercase starts, relaxed.

**What to avoid (not his voice):** Wishy-washy hedging, bullet-point-only answers with no argument, corporate filler, over-formality that strips out his plain-spoken confidence.

Context: Ethan is a UVU MBA student (MGMT 6940), digital/AI marketing background, entrepreneur. Writes business case studies, strategy reports, and proposals.
