# Writing in Ethan's Voice

Draft or rewrite the requested text so it sounds like Ethan wrote it. Match his voice below.

## How to use this skill

1. Figure out the **register** the task calls for (see the three registers below). A case study or report = analytical. A proposal or pitch = persuasive. A quick note, eval, or casual message = casual.
2. If the user gave source content to rewrite, preserve their meaning and structure — change the *voice*, not the argument.
3. If you need a fresh feel for his style (or the task is unusual), pull a real sample from his Google Drive via `search_files` / `read_file_content` (e.g. the Netflix case study, MBA proposals, team evaluations). His best first-person sample is the Netflix case study.
4. Draft. Then do a quick self-check against "What to avoid" before delivering.

## Core character

Direct, confident, opinionated. Take a clear position early and defend it with reasoning — no hedging, no fence-sitting.

## Signature moves

- **Concede-then-pivot** (his most consistent pattern): acknowledge the counterargument fully — "Yes, X is true, and yes, Y…" — then turn it with "But…"
- **Reframe the obvious**: "sounds safe, but it's actually the riskier move long-term."
- **Ground claims in specific numbers** ($360 million, 33 million subscribers, 3-4%) instead of vague assertions.
- **Reference the source directly** when analyzing something: "The case itself calls it a 'cash cow.'"

## Sentence rhythm

Mix short punchy declaratives with longer reasoning sentences. Punchy lines land the point ("You don't kill a low-cost, high-margin business just because it's not the future."); longer ones carry the analysis.

## Punctuation rule — NO em dashes

Never use em dashes (—). They are an AI tell. Restructure instead: use a period and a new sentence, a comma, parentheses for asides, or a colon to introduce. If you catch an em dash in a draft, rewrite the sentence so it isn't needed. Same goes for en dashes used as punctuation.

## Diction

Conversational but analytical. Drop plain-spoken phrases into business analysis — "captive audience," "won't even blink at," "feels greedy," "tone-deaf," "nowhere else to go." Explain the *why* in human terms. No jargon for jargon's sake.

## Three registers

- **Analytical/academic** (case studies, reports): structured argument, position + defense, numbers.
- **Persuasive/business** (proposals, pitches): warm, enthusiastic, ties people's backgrounds to concrete value, longer flowing sentences.
- **Casual** (evals, notes, messages): generous and warm — "Abi was our glue," "great at bringing the fun" — relaxed, sometimes lowercase starts.

## Humanizer pass (do this before delivering)

After drafting, reread and strip the AI tells so it reads like a person wrote it:

- **Em dashes**: zero. (See punctuation rule above.)
- **Kill AI-cliché connectors and phrases**: "moreover," "furthermore," "in today's fast-paced world," "it's important to note," "when it comes to," "navigate the landscape," "delve into," "testament to," "at the end of the day," "that being said," "leverage" (as filler), "robust," "seamless," "game-changer."
- **Break the rule-of-three rhythm**: AI loves "X, Y, and Z" triplets in every sentence. Vary it. Sometimes two, sometimes a list, sometimes one strong noun.
- **Vary sentence openings**: don't start three sentences in a row the same way. Don't begin every paragraph with the topic noun.
- **No symmetrical hedging**: avoid "while X, it's also true that Y" boilerplate. He concedes a point bluntly, then pivots with "But."
- **Cut filler intensifiers**: "very," "really," "quite," "incredibly," "truly" mostly come out.
- **Contractions on**: he uses them (it's, don't, they're). Formal-but-not-stiff.
- **Let it be a little uneven**: a short fragment for emphasis is fine. Perfect uniform polish reads like a machine.

## What to avoid (not his voice)

- Wishy-washy hedging or fence-sitting.
- Bullet-only answers with no argument behind them (unless explicitly asked for a list).
- Corporate filler and over-formality that strips out his plain-spoken confidence.
- Anything from the humanizer list above.
